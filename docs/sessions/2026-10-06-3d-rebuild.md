# 2026-10-06 — 3D rebuild

## Topic

Studied the real Over the Hill footage, concluded the 2D canvas could not reach its
look, and, with the owner's go-ahead, rebuilt the game in 3D on three.js.

## Decisions made

- **The art-direction doc had Over the Hill's palette wrong.** It said "muted,
  desaturated, sage and olive". The footage (both Steam trailers frame by frame, plus 14
  store screenshots, Steam app 2929250) shows a *saturated but narrow* palette: orange
  grass, lilac-grey rock, near-black pines, golden larches. Warm haze toward one sky
  colour holds it together. "When in doubt, desaturate" is replaced by "few hues per
  scene, let the haze unify them". `docs/art-direction.md` is rewritten from the
  footage.
- **Go 3D, with three.js as the only new runtime dependency** (ADR
  `docs/decisions/0005-go-3d-with-threejs.md`). The owner wants "open world driving, no
  multiplayer, not even a game really, but feel like Over the Hill" and explicitly said
  dropping 2D is fine. Still banned without a new ADR: game engines, physics engines,
  react-three-fiber, post-processing stacks. Plain `THREE.WebGLRenderer`.
- **The physics stays ours, pure and unit-tested.** `step(car, input, dt, ground)` takes
  a `Ground` interface (`height`, `obstaclesNear`, `water`, `limit`), so tests use a
  flat plane or a ramp. The model: a point moving along its heading, plus springs for
  body height, pitch and roll chasing the four wheel contacts. Bicycle-model steering.
  Gravity along the slope. A suspension that only pushes, so crests give airtime.
  Heavier gravity in the air (`AIR_GRAVITY = 1.6`). Tilt clamped to `MAX_TILT = 0.7`
  rad so the truck never flips ("not a game about crashing"). Two-circle collision
  against trees and boulders with `sy > 1.1`. Fording up to `MAX_WADE = 1.3` m; deeper
  water stops forward motion but leaves ±1.5 m/s so you can still steer along the shore.
- **One deterministic valley, no asset files.** 2400 m square, 300×300 heightfield (8 m
  cells), seeded simplex noise. It has rolling hills, ridged rocky outcrops, two lake
  basins (`LAKES` in `world.ts`) and a mountain rim from r≈780 m that is both horizon and
  boundary (`WORLD.edge = 900`, hard clamp at `WORLD.limit = 1120`). The spawn at
  `START` (0,0) is flattened into a shelf and faces lake 0. Generation takes ~200 ms.
- **`sampleGrid()` must match the mesh triangulation.** Each cell splits along its
  10–01 diagonal: triangles (00,01,10) and (10,01,11). `buildTerrain` in `scene.ts`
  indexes `[a, d, b, b, d, d+1]` to match. If you change one, change both.
- **Look choices**, each tried against screenshots:
  - Terrain is smooth-shaded with per-vertex colour. The first flat-shaded per-face
    version read as sand dunes or camouflage.
  - Rocks, trees and bushes are flat-shaded.
  - `FogExp2` density 0.0016 in `PALETTE.fog`, under a gradient sky dome with a sun
    glow shader.
  - Low sun `SUN_DIR`. The shadow box (±55 m) follows the car, snapped to texels.
  - High chase camera: about 11.5 m back and 8.5 m up, easing after the heading.
- **Grass is instanced tufts within 62 m of the car only**, re-laid when the car moves
  more than 6 m, with wind sway injected via `onBeforeCompile`. Blades are built with
  both windings and up-normals. `DoubleSide` made back faces render as dark spikes.
- **Trees, bushes and rocks are instanced in 400 m tiles** (`tiled()` in `scene.ts`) so
  whole tiles frustum-cull, including from the shadow pass. Pines and larches share one
  geometry (crown vertex colour white, tinted by `instanceColor`; `tone < 0.16` means a
  larch).
- **HUD:** title, a compass strip (north = −z, east = +x; bearing = π − heading), a
  fading hint, and the same touch buttons. No speedo, no minimap.

Verified: `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm test` (20 passing) and `pnpm build`
all pass. Drove it in headless Chromium (SwiftShader) via Playwright: spawn, cornering,
climbing, the lake and cresting into the valley all render correctly. A 4×240 s soak of
blind driving over the real terrain found no NaNs, no flips, max tilt 40° and airtime
≤ 2 s. The bot stalls only against tree trunks, because it never reverses.

**Nothing is committed yet.** Work is on local branch `feat/game-3d`, off `main`.

## Open questions

- **Real-device frame rate is unmeasured.** Headless SwiftShader runs at ~3 fps, which
  says nothing. The scene is ~0.8M triangles before culling. Is it smooth on the
  owner's phone and laptop?
- Should the gameplay sun be backlit (sun ahead, glowing horizon, the trailers' most
  striking shots) instead of side-lit? Side-lit was chosen for readability.
- The grass reads more as texture than tall grass. Taller and denser, at what cost?
- Is the 17 m/s (~61 km/h) top speed and ~6 s coast-down the right calm?
- Push `feat/game-3d` and open a PR? It wasn't asked for yet. A push would produce a
  Vercel preview URL to try on a phone.

## Exact next step

Get `feat/game-3d` onto a Vercel preview and drive it on a real phone and laptop, then
tune performance:

1. If not done, commit (`feat(game): rebuild in 3D on three.js`) and
   `git push -u origin feat/game-3d`, and get the preview URL from the Vercel
   deployment for project `gilbyy-web`.
2. On a phone, check the frame rate (add a temporary `?fps` overlay in `Game.tsx` if
   needed, then remove it). If it's under ~45 fps, pull these levers in this order:
   - `RADIUS`/`SPACING` in `buildGrass` (`scene.ts`)
   - `renderer.setPixelRatio` cap (1.75 → 1.25 on touch devices)
   - `sun.shadow.mapSize` (2048 → 1024)
   - the tree chance in `buildWorld` (`forestAt(...) * 0.85`)
3. Then start time of day: animate `SUN_DIR`, the sky uniforms and the fog colour
   through dusk to night, and add headlight `SpotLight`s on the car in
   `car-model.ts`.
